Corrections of the first reading ('probe collapses to chance across robots; the regime where a certificate should refuse'):
1. The collapse is not uniform: AUC ranges from .38 to .86; only sirius->droid and droid->mutex are at chance, mutex->droid is significantly below .5 (consistent with an absence-versus-presence cue in mutex, untested). A global polarity flip is not the explanation (droid cells are .38, .53, .49, .65 and droid is readable within dataset). The shift looks like an embodiment shift (pad colour and shape differ).
2. The collapse depends on the probe recipe: with a stronger regularisation (C = .001, chosen after seeing results with target labels, exploratory) mutex->sirius rises to .81, sirius->mutex to .90, droid->sirius to .75, while cells involving droid stay at .42-.56.
3. The 'informative' gate rejected a cell where repair helps: sirius->mutex has AUC .858 and Q recovers 88% of the R0-oracle gap, but it fails the gate because the time-only tree (.252) is close; the gate as registered excludes exactly some cells where repair could be tested.
4. Q knew the prior here: balancing makes target prevalence exactly .5, so Q equals the median target score. The 88% is not evidence for Q under unknown prevalence, and this study does not test Proposition C'.
5. 'A certificate should refuse here' is untested: no refusal rule was evaluated; a label-free dataset-identity detector fires on every pair including the one where repair worked.
6. C4 failed because of episode clustering, not shift: in mutex->sirius audit error .356 vs evaluation .384, intraclass correlation about .20, design effect about 8; frame-level Clopper-Pearson coverage falls with m (QA .93, .86, .77, .59 at m = 20, 50, 100, 200); a single pool and a single informative pair, so diagnostic only.
Allowed reading: with frozen DINOv2-small CLS+mean features and a linear probe, gripper state is read almost perfectly within a robot dataset (AUC .95-1.00) but transfers inconsistently across three wrist-camera datasets (AUC .38-.86); in the cells where transfer fails no threshold rule can help (consistent with the T5 floor, which holds in 9 of 9 cells but is valid for any score); in one cell with AUC .86 quantile matching recovered most of the threshold error when the prevalence was known. This is a descriptive study on three datasets chosen by us (two others were dropped on a one-observer visual check), reused from earlier H84-H96 work, with class balancing that removes prior shift. It is not confirmatory and not Tier 1.
Not claimed: that frozen probes cannot transfer across robots in general; that certificates should refuse in these cells; anything about unknown prevalence; any support for or against Proposition C'.
