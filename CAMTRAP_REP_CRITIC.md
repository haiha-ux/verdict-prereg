## 3. C4 (betting vs CP)
Computation (ct_run.py:154-173): per replicate ONE audit pool (7x5=35 sites cct, 50 wel/ss3); 2000 draws of k sites WITHOUT replacement from it; truth = pooled R0 error on the eval frames of all folds (disjoint sites from the audit pool, the same site population). Coverage in 15b = share of the 2000 subsamples whose bound >= truth, then averaged/counted over 20 replicates. Your questions:
- Independence of the 20 replicates: the audit pools are random 1/3 subsets of the same 110/150 sites (overlap = random level, .32-.34) and truth is the same population with eval sets overlapping 68%: truth SD across replicates only .004-.010 (error ~.06-.18). So the 20 replicates give 20 DIFFERENT but strongly overlapping audit pools against an almost constant truth. They are genuine evidence about how coverage varies with the random audit-pool draw (that is exactly what the single pool of 14d lacked), but they are dependent (same sites/frames/features, truth nearly fixed) and speak about this site population only; effective n is well below 20 and no CI over replicates should be quoted as if independent.
- Betting 1.0: U/truth min over reps = 3.9 (cct k=5), 1.8 (cct k=20), 3.2/1.6 (wel k=5/20), 8.3/3.3 (ss3). A bound 1.6-8x the truth in EVERY replicate is nominally >= .95 whatever its true level (a bound at 50% nominal level would probably still cover 1.0). So 20/20 says "no failure seen", not "valid at .95" and not "tight". Validity of the bound is the Waudby-Smith-Ramdas / theory-82 theorem (needs iid-site sampling), not something this run tests.
- CP: within one replicate the cover value is mostly determined by the audit-pool offset (pool mean error minus truth), because the k=20 subsample of a 35-site pool nearly equals the pool (cp_cover per replicate ranges .11-.99 at k=20, .47-.88 at cct k=5). Hence the per-replicate rule ">=19 of 20 reps >= .95" (15) is a poor statistic for CP: a valid frame-level bound would also show varying per-pool coverage. The MEAN over replicates (.67-.89), which averages the pool draw, is the meaningful number: coverage of the site-population error by a frame-level CP bound from k random sites, which is fair as a target (truth = same site population, other sites) and which falls with k (design-effect signature: n_eff clusters << frames). The size depends on the population heterogeneity of these three sets (cct has the largest between-site spread); do not generalise the numbers.
- Fairness of "CP undercovers": fair as a statement that frame-iid binomial bounds on clustered camera-trap frames are anti-conservative for the site-population error. It is a textbook cluster-sampling fact, not a finding about VERDICT; the claim "cluster-level bound valid" rests on the WSR theorem plus 0 failures seen at 1.6-10x slack.
- Not tested: coverage when k audit sites are drawn from a pool whose size is much larger than k, and betting coverage at tight bounds (n < 5 sites, other alpha) where failure would be informative. An informative test would use alpha = .5 or .8 (nominal level lowered until failures appear) -- optional, would need a new lock.
