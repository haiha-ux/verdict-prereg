## (5) What the temporal branch (Amendments 17-25) can honestly claim
On two UT Austin robot datasets, with a frozen DINOv2-small probe, ranking between-node frames by a recency-discounted margin (hold_c; k=1 Lipschitz margin is not better) beats plain hold and EMA at 2.5/1.25 Hz against the reader's own output, replicated on two fresh samples; the benefit is the age covariate, not the Lipschitz theory (a logistic ranker with age equals it), and it is not certified risk and not novel. Against ground-truth labels the picture is: sensor labels mixed (hold ahead at mutex in the second sample), expert surgical labels (cataract) recency discounting is worse than smoothing, which in turn is better than plain hold; on THUMOS14 human labels plain hold beats hold_c (significantly at m=8) and both beat EMA, with a reader that barely beats a constant. On THUMOS14 the age-discount again wins only against the reader's own output (-.012/-.021 AURC, ub<0), i.e. it better predicts the reader's flicker, not the truth. The regime selector (rho>=2) is one post hoc statistic fitted to 4 values and tested on one near-cut point (2.08, eval 1.95); it is not confirmed and S1 fails under either selection. No domain has a reader good enough (all failed or noisy) to say anything about correctness of held decisions; the defensible paper claim is "temporal margin ordering predicts a frozen reader's own unobserved decisions better when discounted by age", with the honest negative that this does not carry to human/expert labels on two non-robot domains.
