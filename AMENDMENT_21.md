# 99. Test 5 (exploratory second stage): the maximum-ratio margin of the cone intersection (design and lock, 2026-10-28, BEFORE the run)
STATUS: locked before `tc5_rc.py` was run. This is informed by the result of test 4 (theory/98 addendum 20b: the additive-distance margin of cone_all lost to the age-discounted hold); the diagnosis (the correct sweep for the lambda-scaled cone intersection is the maximum ratio |z_i - tau| / (e + L|t - t_i|) over nodes) was derived from the structure of Theorem 3 and then written here; it is a second stage, not a fresh pre-registration. No novelty claim (set-membership estimation; age-discounted confidence).
Data, calibration and metrics: as theory/98 (z_<ds>.npz, dev_calib for e, L = M_1 at q = 1.0 V1, and the age-discount parameter c*; between-node labelled frames, m in {2, 4, 8}, causal rules, 9 cells; AURC against the reader's own output and the sensor label; dev-calibrated operating points at eps in {0, .002, .01}).
Methods: cone_max (margin = max over all past nodes of the ratio; decision = side of the maximising node), cone_max_w5 (last 5 nodes), cert_k1 (last-node ratio), hold_c (|z_last - tau| / (age + c*)), hold.
Predictions (scored to the letter): (a) cone_max has a reader-target AURC <= cert_k1 in >= 7 of 9 cells (cells in which all methods coincide count as ties, not wins; wins are required in >= 4 cells with m in {4, 8}). (d) cone_max_w5 and cone_max have AURC within 5% (relative) in >= 8 of 9 cells.
Not claimed: certified decisions about the true state; novelty; any benefit against the sensor label; Tier-1; A*. Same dependence caveats as before; three robot gripper datasets.

Theorem 3 (exact attainable set) is unaffected: it says which decisions are sound over the class, not that any sweep of the intersection gives the best risk-coverage order. Tests 4 and 5 are not independent of each other (test 5 was designed after seeing test 4).
Net outcome of the development of the hypothesis (tests 1-5): a valid conditional enclosure rule with a proved exact characterisation for the Lipschitz class; on three robot gripper datasets the best simple rule is a hold discounted by the age of the last observation, the set-membership refinements do not improve risk-coverage, higher order does not help, SPRT and median filters are much worse.
