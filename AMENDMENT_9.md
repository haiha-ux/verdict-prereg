1. HIGH: the audit-sample bounds only cover the mean error of the AUDIT pool, while the truth is the error on a different EVALUATION pool, and the difference between the two pools changes sign (gripper R0: audit .062, eval .093; valve R0: audit .187, eval .134). So a part of the under-coverage of T and F is a pool difference, not a defect of the interval. At m = 200 the sample is 40 of 50 audit episodes drawn without replacement while T, F, H use no finite-population factor, which also lifts T to .91; with replacement T reaches only .85-.94.
2. 'T fails because e is small' is right but incomplete: with the truth set to the audit mean (no pool difference) T covers .37-.79 at e = 4, .63-.88 at e = 8, .78-.95 at e = 20, and .91-.94 at e = 200 with replacement; a Wald t-interval on rare, skewed errors (rate about .05, sd .12-.23) needs e of about 100 or more. Clustering is real (ICC .25-.46: frame-level F with replacement stops at .85-.87).

F is edge-sensitive (a change of .002 in the truth moves coverage from .81 to 1.0, drawer R0A at m = 50), so P1's near-miss is noise, and 11 of 23 failing F cells are gripper.
